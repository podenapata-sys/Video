## Clean VO block (paste into TTS)

If you ever wake up at 3:00 a.m.… …and there is a deer standing in your yard, leave the house. Do not turn on the lights. Do not wake anyone. Do not wait for sunrise. We do not mean a deer that walks. Or one that eats. We mean one that stands completely still. Head raised. Ears forward. Facing your house. You will think it is looking at you. It is not looking at your window. It is looking at what is behind you. Count to ten. If it has not moved, there are seven rules.

Rule one. Do not turn on the lights. You will want a better look. Don't. What it is watching does not know you are awake. Turn on the light, and it will. Deer are prey animals. When they sense danger, they freeze and stare at it. Then they run. This one will not. Not for a car. Not for a falling branch. Not for your knock on the glass. Whatever it is watching is closer than any of those.

Rule two. Watch the head. Not the eyes. The head. If it points at the kitchen, stay out of the kitchen. If it turns to the hallway, do not enter the hallway. If it rises toward your window, step away from the glass. It is not following you. It is following something inside. Do not be standing where it stops.

Rule three. If it stamps, freeze. One strike. Hard. Against the ground. Stop. Do not move. Do not speak. Do not turn around. The stamp means something is close enough to react to. You may hear breathing. The floor may creak. You may feel something close enough to touch your shoulder. Ignore it. At the second strike, walk to the nearest exit. If the second strike comes from inside the house, you waited too long.

Rule four. If someone calls your name, do not answer. This is the rule most people break. It will sound like your mother. From the kitchen. Or your brother. Asking why you are awake. The voice will be normal. Listen to the words. It will not tell you to come down. It will tell you to stay. "It's cold outside." "Go back to bed." Ignore it, and it gets angry. Then scared. Then it cries. Do not answer. It knows you are leaving. It is running out of ways to stop you.

Rule five. If the deer walks toward the house, you have one minute. It does not walk like a deer. The legs move slowly. The body does not move at all. The head does not bob. The eyes do not blink. The hooves make no sound. One woman saw it from her bedroom window. It stood under the same tree for almost an hour. At exactly 3:17, it took one step. Her husband laughed. He went downstairs to scare it off. He reached the bottom step. Then he stopped. She called his name. He did not answer. Then his voice came from the kitchen. And from the stairs. At the same time. She left through the window. The police found every light in the house on. They did not find her husband. Something upstairs was still calling her name.

Rule six. If the deer is facing the front door, do not use it. Back door. Garage. Window. Never walk into what the deer is watching. Listen before you open anything. Scratching means find another way. Knocking means do not answer. Your own voice asking to be let in means get away from the door. By now, it has heard you talk. It has heard you walk. It has learned your breathing. It is getting better at it.

Rule seven. Once you are outside, do not look back. Keep walking. Past the driveway. Past the car. If the front door opens behind you, keep walking. There may be footsteps. Someone may shout that you forgot something. Only at the road do you look at the deer. If it is still in the yard, you are too close. Keep going. If it is gone, you are not safe. Check the windows. Briefly. Look for someone standing behind the glass.

We left one thing out of this file. Survivors check their cameras the next morning. Every recording shows the same thing. The deer was facing one window before anyone had walked into that room. Look closer. Something was already standing behind the glass. Watching the deer. Same height as you. Same clothes. When you walked to the window, it stepped back into the dark. That is why the deer never moved. It had been standing in your house for hours. Practicing being you. So if you see a deer in your yard at 3:00 a.m., leave. And if your own voice calls you back inside, keep walking.
